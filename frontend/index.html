Presentation Slideshow

Browse through key insights and designs from the project pitch presentation (AssessIQ.pptx).

Slide 1: Problem Statement

The Bottleneck of Legacy Proctoring

Standard online examination proctoring streams raw, high-definition video directly to centralized servers for analysis. This consumes massive network bandwidth, raises major compliance and privacy concerns, and results in extremely high cloud compute/GPU costs for institutions.

Slide 2: Technical Approach

Edge-Based Machine Learning

AssessIQ performs real-time FaceMesh and object detection directly inside the examinee's browser using MediaPipe and TensorFlow.js. Video frames stay on the client's device; only lightweight JSON telemetry packets (focus loss, phone presence) are transmitted over WebSockets, saving 99% bandwidth.

Slide 3: Infinite Variation

Dynamic Question Bank & Semantic Evaluation

Instead of relying on standard static question banks prone to leakages, AssessIQ links with high-speed LLMs (LLaMA 3 / Gemini) to dynamically generate unique challenges. Descriptive written answers are evaluated semantically against marking rubrics, grading and returning reviews in under a second.

Slide 4: System Architecture

Built for Scalability

The platform features a decoupled FastAPI backend, standard WebSockets for alerts, and SQLite with WAL enabled to achieve rapid concurrency during peak test sessions. native background tasks and caching engines support sub-50ms server responses.

Help Center

Frequently Asked Questions

Explore answers to common questions about our architecture, privacy models, and performance.

How does client-side proctoring protect student privacy? ▼
Unlike standard systems that upload your webcam stream to enterprise servers, AssessIQ performs the computer vision calculations directly inside your browser. No video frames leave your device. Only simple alert packets, like "anomaly: student focus loss", are transmitted via WebSockets to notify the educator.
Is AI semantic grading consistent and fair? ▼
Yes. AssessIQ evaluates descriptive/essay questions based on semantic token mappings and concept checks, comparing student answers against educator rubrics. It scores based on conceptual understanding rather than keyword stuffing, eliminating standard human grading bias and fatigue.
Does proctoring work on low-spec hardware? ▼
Yes. The models used (MediaPipe FaceMesh and Coco-SSD) are compile-optimized for WebGL acceleration. They utilize the candidate's built-in graphics engine, achieving smooth 25-30 frames per second checks on average modern notebooks without causing CPU slowdowns.
